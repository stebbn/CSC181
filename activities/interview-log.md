### Questions & Responses

**Is this your first time visiting Iligan, or do you come here often?&nbsp;**

- It’s my 3rd time visiting, but recently I’ve been living here permanently.&nbsp;

**How do you usually get around when you're in a city you're not familiar with?&nbsp;**

- I use Google Maps and ask local residents for directions.&nbsp;

**When you arrived here, how did you figure out which jeepney, bus, or tricycle to take from the terminal or drop-off point?&nbsp;**

- I checked Google Maps, asked the driver to let me know when to get off, or asked the person I was riding with.&nbsp;

**What do you do when a driver or local uses local terminology or landmarks you've never heard of?**

- I either look it up on Google Maps or ask local residents to clarify.

**Have you ever ended up on the wrong jeepney or taken a route going the opposite direction because you weren't sure of the landmark stops? What happened?&nbsp;**

- Yes. One time, I was heading toward Mahayahay near the Shell station, but I mistakenly thought it was a Petron station. I trusted the bus conductor to drop me off at Petron, which resulted in me alighting a few kilometers away from my actual destination. Once I realized the mistake, I had to catch another jeepney that actually ran past the Shell station.

**How do you figure out how much fare to pay when you're boarding a vehicle here for the first time?&nbsp;**

- I usually ask the driver or conductor directly, or I just hand over the standard minimum student fare.&nbsp;

**If your trip requires transferring to another jeepney or bus, how do you know where to get off and where to ride the next one?**

- I check the signboards/route listings painted on the sides of the jeepneys.&nbsp;

**Have you ever felt like you overpaid or were unsure if you received the right change because you didn't know the standard fare matrix?&nbsp;**

- Yes, definitely.&nbsp;

**When navigating unfamiliar routes right now, do you rely on apps like Google Maps, asking locals on the street, or just asking the driver?&nbsp;**

- I use a combination of all three depending on the situation.&nbsp;

**If there were a simple tool that showed you exact jeepney routes, transfer points, and calculated fares upfront without requiring you to know local landmarks, would that be useful during your trip?&nbsp;**

- Absolutely, 100%.&nbsp;

### Key Takeaways & Surprises

- **What surprised us:** The user depends on Google Maps for route navigation, whereas local commuters can refer to landmarks. In addition, they devise ways to interpret visual images, like reading the hand-painted route signboards on the sides of the moving jeepneys to guess transfer points instead of standard maps or schedules.  
- **Primary Pain Point Confirmed:** Due to the unfamiliarity with local landmark nomenclature and the official fare matrix, there is high vulnerability to misdirection and overcharging, such as being dropped off kilometres away wrongly for a Shell station instead of a Petron station.

### Impact on Project Proposal

- **Problem Statement Revision:** Confirmed that non-native residents and newcomers face significant navigation hurdles because local public transit operates strictly on informal, landmark-based cues and unwritten fare norms rather than structured map routes.  
- **Scope & Feature Adjustments:**  
  * Implement clear visual landmark tags alongside digital route mapping to prevent drop-off errors.  
  * Integrate an automated fare calculator with student/discount options based on distance to eliminate fare uncertainty.  
  * Provide explicit transfer point guides rather than forcing users to rely on side-panel jeepney text.  
- **Assumptions Validated:**&nbsp;  
  * Visitors and new residents struggle with landmark-only directions and are eager to adopt an upfront route-and-fare app.  
  * Overpaying or uncertainty around standard fare matrices is a prevalent issue for commuters unfamiliar with local transit systems.

## Senior Citizen / PWD / Students

### Participant Profile

- **Name**: Madam Gloria Macapagal Arroyo (Pseudonym)  
- **Stakeholder Category**: Primary Users (Senior Citizen)  
- **Interviewer**: Rey Mark Degamo  
- **Date & Time**: October 23, 2026; 5:12pm  
- **Duration**: 41min  
- **Setting**: Outside their house (porch); purok 3 mimbalot, buru-un  
- **Method**: Informal Interview

### Questions & Responses (All answer are summary and translated)

**How long did you live in the Iligan area?**

- I was born here and grew here until I became married then moved to Cebu and now I am old and have successful children, I move back here.

**As someone who lives outside the main city, how often do you go to the city?**

- Mostly every Sunday I buy school supplies for my grandchildren and also some groceries. There are also days I need to go on weekdays if my grandchildren need urgent school materials.

**Since you only go to the city once or twice a week, how do you know how much the jeepney or bus fare is?**

- Most of the time when I ride a jeepney I always go near the front to ask the driver directly how much the fare for seniors, for my age I sometimes forget the exact fare so to be fair I always ask. But sometimes I sit in the back, I can't ask the driver and no conductor. I just pass the money and say ‘senior’.

**Do you get tired or uneasy when you ride and don't know the exact price, mostly for seniors?**

- Since I am now old, I can easily forget that's why I just ask every time. It's not tiring to ask but not easy every time, sometimes it's hard to ask and sometimes if the jeep for some reason maybe they can hear me, I don't get discounted.

**If you are worried about not getting a discount why not just ready your fare payment in advance?**

- You know, buses and jeep have different fares, when Miltrasco appears their fare is much more expensive compared to Donsal and jeep, so I avoid Miltrasco. Then after like a year when I was forced to ride Miltrasco, it was cheaper, but later next ride it was more expensive. So a safer choice is to just bring the whole money instead of coins.

**Do you experience a lying driver telling you the wrong price?**

- I don't know if they are lying but like last month, first week I asked for a price it was 22 for senior then next week it became 23 then another week it came back to 22, so maybe the 23 one lies, also some driver dont give me senior discount even if I said I was senior, maybe they did not hear me or maybe not enough passenger to give me discount.

**Do you feel unfair about not being able to get a discount?**

- Not really much since they are just trying to earn a living, but at least for seniors, pwd or students they give the proper discounted fare price.

### Key Takeaways & Surprises

**What surprised us**

- Fares differ by operator. Miltrasco was assumed to be pricier than Donsal and the jeepneys.  
- Not every driver gives the senior discount even when it is requested. She is unsure whether they didn’t hear her, or whether there were too few passengers to bother, so the discount feels optional.&nbsp;  
- The discounted fare changed from 22 to 23 and back to 22 within weeks, with no explanation or notice.

**Primary Pain Point Confirmed:**&nbsp;

- Seniors have no reliable way to know the correct fare and discount before riding. She depends on asking the driver, which fails when she sits at the back, there is no conductor, or the driver can’t hear her. She also carries whole bills instead of coins as a workaround.&nbsp;

### Impact on Project Proposal

- **Problem Statement Revision:** Add that fares vary by operator (bus vs. jeepney) and change without notice, and that discounts are applied inconsistently, so passengers cannot verify what they should pay.  
- **Scope & Feature Adjustments:**  
  * Fare lookup per operator and route, showing regular and discounted fares side by side.  
  * Large, simple text for elderly users.  
  * Display the “last updated” date of each fare.  
- **Assumptions Validated:**&nbsp;  
  * Seniors currently rely on asking drivers: validated.  
  * Discounted fares are unclear and inconsistent: validated.  
  * Still to verify: whether the 22 vs. 23 difference is a real fare change or a driver error (check with LTFRB or the city transport office).

### Participant Profile

- **Name:** Work Pilgrim (Pseudonym)  
- **Origin / Home Town:** Region XII, Philippines  
- **Purpose of Visit:** Work / Guardianship  
- **Familiarity with Iligan Routes:** \[ \] First-time visitor  \|  \[ \] Occasional visitor  \|  \[X\] Frequent visitor

### Questions & Responses (All answer are summary and translated)

**Can you tell me a little about yourself and what you do for work?**

- &nbsp;I’m a salesman for X company. I visit stores and convince owners to take us as their supplier. I started in the warehouse as a helper, then moved to the field because I could drive.

**What does a typical week look like, and which areas do you cover?**&nbsp;

- Currently, I work in R10 and R12, but I used to work in R9. I spend about 20 hours a week on the road, sometimes more. On long days I drive around 8 hours with stops for gas, coffee, and stretching. Region 9 is the hardest because the towns are far apart and the roads are rough.

**When you go somewhere new, how do you find a specific address?**

- I start with Google Maps. If the store isn’t on the map, I call the owner or ask people on the road. Locals mostly give landmarks, like “after the bridge, turn at the chapel, next to the bakery.” If I don’t know the landmark, I ask someone else, and if that fails I call the owner again.

**Has a direction or landmark ever led you to the wrong place?**

- Yes. I was told “near the school,” but the barangay had two schools, an elementary and a high school, and I went to the wrong one. I called the owner and lost about an hour, and the store was almost closing.

**When you commute somewhere new, how do you know which ride to take, where to get off, and where to transfer?**

- It depends on the location. Some region has jeepneys, while some primarily rely on tricycles and sikads. Asking the drivers or the person beside me often works out well, but miscommunication is a risk.

**How do you know how much to pay the first time you ride a route? Have you ever paid more than you should have?**

- Again just ask the driver. When it’s unclear, I’d offer a lowball to the drivers, and they’d usually argue with the right price, but there’s no shortage of bad actors in this world. I’ve overpaid on a few tricycles rides in a new area because I didn’t know the right fare.

**Have you noticed fares differing between operators or changing on the same route?**&nbsp;

- Yes. Buses cost more than jeepneys, and fares on the same route change. The driver only said fuel prices went up, and nothing was announced. Fares also differ by region, and I think tricycles cost more, but I’m not too sure.

**Does anyone in your family commute regularly?**&nbsp;

- My son is a college student in Iligan and rides a jeepney every day. He would ask me at first, but now he seems to have familiarized himself well. In his first week he rode the wrong jeepney, and I had to pick him up.

**What do you use to find your way around, and how is the signal?**&nbsp;

- I use google maps since its the most used by my coworkers. It works even offline, at least the tracking location function, so signal is a non-issue. The only problem is when I’m in unupdated roads. Before a trip I call the store owner and screenshot the map for confirmation, and if I lose signal I ask people. When the app and locals disagree, I trust the locals, Maps sometimes sends me down roads that are barely passable.

**Is there anything about getting around that we haven’t talked about?**

- The hardest part is being new to a place and not knowing the names of places.

### Key Takeaways & Surprises

**What surprised us**

- He trusts locals over Google Maps because outdated roads send him down barely passable routes.  
- He lowballs fares and waits for the driver to counter.  
- Signal is a non-issue because Maps location tracking works offline.  
- Available transport depends on the region (jeepneys in some, tricycles and sikads in others).  
- A driver blamed a fare change on fuel prices, and nothing was announced.

**Primary Pain Point Confirmed:**&nbsp;

- Newcomers can’t verify places or fares without asking someone. Landmark directions can be ambiguous (two schools in one barangay cost him an hour), fares are learned by asking or haggling, and map apps have outdated roads.&nbsp;

### Impact on Project Proposal

- **Problem Statement Revision:** Add work travelers as affected users. Add that fares are learned by asking or haggling with drivers.&nbsp;&nbsp;  
- **Scope & Feature Adjustments:**  
  * Landmark tags that show the place type or nearest cross-street for same-name places (e.g., “Elementary School” vs. “High School”).  
  * Fare calculator that gives a reference price before haggling.  
  * Out of scope for now: tricycle and sikad fares.  
- **Assumptions Validated:**&nbsp;  
  * Travelers unfamiliar with an area rely on asking people for directions and fares: validated.  
  * Still to verify: whether fare changes are real or driver error (the driver cited fuel prices; check with LTFRB or the city transport office).  
  * Still to verify: how much signal loss affects users in transit (he says signal is a non-issue but asks people when he loses it).

### Questions & Responses (All answer are summary and translated)

**As a student boarder from Gensan, how has your overall commuting experience in Iligan been compared to back home?**

- Honestly, commuting here in Iligan is actually a bit better and more straightforward than in Gensan. Since we had to rely on motorcycles back home and pay special rates which wasn't ideal.

**When you first moved here for school, how did you figure out which jeepneys to take around the city?**

- It was trial and error plus asking my classmates who are locals. I definitely rode the wrong jeep once because I didn't realize that two jeepneys with similar names were bound for completely different inner barangays like in Hinaplanon or Luinab.

**Do you regularly avail of the student fare discount when commuting to campus or around town?**

- I try to, especially on daily trips to campus since every peso matters on my case. But honestly, it depends on where I'm sitting. If I'm right behind the driver, I hand over the exact discounted fare. But if I'm at the very back, it's awkward to yell "student" across ten people, so I often just pay the regular adult fare to avoid the hassle.

**How do you determine the exact student discounted fare, especially for longer distances?**

- I usually just guess based on what my boardmates and friends told me, but I'm never 100% sure about the actual LTFRB fare matrix. Sometimes I hand over a 20-peso coin expecting a change, but the driver hands back less. I rarely complain or clarify because I don't want to be dramatic over two or three pesos.

**How do you handle local landmark names when drivers or commuters mention drop-off points?&nbsp;**

- It was a bit confusing when I first arrived. I usually just pay attention to the place or if it is really hard to spot, I usually use Google Maps for help and to be sure.

**Have you ever experienced any confusion from drivers regarding your student discount?**

- Yeah, many times. During weekends or academic breaks, some drivers argue that student discounts don't apply because there's no class. Or sometimes if i'm not wearing my school ID, some drivers just ignore my request for student fare and treat it as a regular payment without giving back proper change.

**If you have to take a trip requiring a transfer, how do you know where to switch jeepneys?**

- I usually ask my boardmates or friends before commuting, and look for jeepney stops to find and ask for my next jeepney ride.

**If there were a mobile tool that mapped out exact jeepney routes, transfer nodes, and automatically calculated the official student discount fare upfront, would that help your daily commute?**

- It would be very helpful, not just for me, but also to every other students that are not from Iligan

### Key Takeaways & Surprises

**What surprised us**

- We assumed out-of-towners would struggle, but students coming from tricycle-heavy cities like Gensan actually find Iligan’s fixed jeepney routes much simpler and cheaper to navigate.&nbsp;  
- Students know they’re legally entitled to a student fare, but they often skip asking for it. Shouting "student" from the back of a packed jeepney or arguing over 1 or 2 pesos change feels too embarrassing.&nbsp;  
- Drivers frequently claim student discounts don't apply on weekends or school breaks, taking advantage of students who don't know the rules.&nbsp;

**Primary Pain Point Confirmed:**&nbsp;

- Non-local students constantly lose money because they aren't sure of exact fare rates, don't recognize local landmark names, and feel uncomfortable asserting their discount in crowded jeeps.&nbsp;

### Impact on Project Proposal

- **Problem Statement Revision:** Update the problem statement to show that while Iligan's jeepney routes are fairly direct, out-of-town students still overpay due to social awkwardness during payment and unfamiliarity with local landmark names.&nbsp;  
- **Scope & Feature Adjustments:**  
  * Add a simple toggle for Regular vs. Student/Senior/PWD fares so users see the exact coin breakdown before they even board.&nbsp;  
  * Include picture-based landmarks for main student drop-offs (like MSU-IIT, Tibanga, and San Miguel) instead of just text names.&nbsp;  
  * Create a clear, high-contrast screen showing the exact fare that students can easily show the driver if there's any dispute.&nbsp;  
- **Assumptions Validated:**&nbsp;  
  * Out-of-town students rely mostly on trial-and-error and asking boardmates to figure out routes.&nbsp;  
  * Confusion over exact discount rates and fear of confrontation lead to regular overpayment among non-local students.&nbsp;
